**Spend this week testing whether coaches have a problem worth paying you to solve. Allocate all 8 hours to customer discovery; spend $0 of the $300.** Your friend’s enthusiasm is encouragement, but it does not establish demand.

“AI dashboard for independent fitness coaches” is too broad to build against yet. Treat next month’s launch as a small paid pilot you could deliver partly by hand.

This week:

1. **Find and contact coaches — 2 hours.** List 15 independent coaches you can reach directly. Send a short, personal message: “I’m exploring ways to reduce fitness coaches’ admin work. Could I ask you about your workflow for 20 minutes? I’m learning, not pitching software.” Aim to book five conversations.

2. **Interview five coaches — 2 hours.** Ask: “Walk me through last week’s client admin.” “What took the most time?” “When did that last cause a problem?” “How do you handle it now?” “What do you currently pay for?” Ask for specific recent examples. Avoid “Would you use an AI dashboard?”

3. **Choose one recurring problem — 1 hour.** Compare the answers. Look for a task multiple coaches already spend time or money addressing. Record frequency, consequences, current workaround, and who wants help. Do not assume AI is necessary.

4. **Draft one narrow pilot offer — 1 hour.** If a clear problem emerges, describe one deliverable, its turnaround time, a fixed price, and a cap of two coaches. Choose something you can reliably deliver within your weekly availability, using manual work where needed.

5. **Offer it and follow up — 2 hours.** Ask relevant interviewees to commit to the paid pilot. A concrete purchase decision gives you stronger evidence than compliments.

**Decision rule:** As a practical threshold, proceed only if at least three coaches independently describe the same recurring pain and one commits to paying for your proposed solution. Otherwise, keep interviewing and postpone the software launch.

This week’s success is a supported problem and a credible paid offer—not a dashboard.
