I recommend taking the spreadsheet walkthrough this Thursday before switching ideas. You have a specific, recurring problem reported independently by three people and an invitation to see the actual workflow. That is worth one more experiment. It is not yet evidence that anyone will pay.

Thirty exciting AI ideas would give you more options, but you already have an opportunity to test demand directly.

Here’s what I’d do next:

1. **Ask the bookkeeper to walk through last Friday.** Where did the two hours go? Which documents were missing? How did they track requests, follow up, and decide what still needed attention? Ask them to show the spreadsheet using anonymized examples.

2. **Offer a small paid pilot.** Propose helping with one Friday’s document chasing, using their existing workflow and keeping them in control of client communications. Agree on the scope, fee, and success measure beforehand—such as reducing their time spent. You can deliver manually; you don’t need to build AI software first.

3. **Make a decision after the test.** Payment plus measurable time saved would justify another pilot. A refusal is useful too: find out whether the problem is insufficiently painful, the offer is wrong, or they don’t trust the proposed approach.

Your boredom also matters. As a solo founder, you’ll have to sustain interest in the customers and their work. Ask yourself whether you dislike bookkeeping customers or simply find early validation less exciting than imagining products. Those point to different decisions.

**Give this one walkthrough and one concrete offer.** If the work still feels unappealing, move on deliberately. You don’t owe this idea a company—but it has earned a cheap test before you replace evidence with novelty.
