**Drop the personally covered, 24/7 five-minute escalation guarantee before selling this.** It conflicts with your full-time job and 10 spare hours a week. AI answering calls does not remove your obligation to respond when it escalates. With no clinics signed, demand is also unproven.

Your next step is to test a narrower service that fits your actual availability:

1. **Interview five clinic decision-makers.** Ask how they handle missed calls, what callers need, which problems cost staff time, and what requires an immediate response. Find out what they would pay to improve. Don’t promise coverage yet.

2. **Offer one bounded administrative use case.** For example: answering clinic-approved FAQs and collecting callback or appointment requests. Clearly distinguish a request from a confirmed booking. Exclude diagnosis, medical advice, and clinical triage. Have the clinic approve instructions for urgent callers; your personal escalation queue should not serve as an emergency pathway.

3. **Make the service promise match available staffing.** You could test automated intake available around the clock, with human follow-up during explicitly agreed hours by clinic staff. Promise five-minute escalation only if a staffed arrangement can actually deliver it and its cost fits the price.

4. **Seek one paid, limited pilot before building broadly.** Agree on scope, human responsibilities, failure handling, and success measures. Before handling patient information, establish the clinic’s privacy and security requirements and confirm the proposed setup meets them.

Use your first week’s 10 hours for outreach, interviews, and a simple pilot offer. During a pilot, measure successful administrative outcomes, errors, escalation frequency, and your total support time. Calculate profit after software, human coverage, and your own labor.
