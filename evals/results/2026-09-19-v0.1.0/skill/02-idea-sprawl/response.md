**I recommend taking Thursday’s spreadsheet walkthrough before abandoning bookkeeping.** Three independent bookkeepers reporting the same weekly task is a useful pain signal. The spreadsheet offer gives you a cheap way to inspect actual behavior. Neither proves willingness to pay.

Your boredom matters: a solo business must be something you can sustain. But one bounded test can distinguish an unattractive business from an idea you simply haven’t tested yet.

Spend at most two hours preparing, meeting, and following up:

- Ask the bookkeeper to walk through last Friday’s chase using their spreadsheet, with client details removed.
- Establish who does the work, who can authorize payment, what they already use, and what happens when documents arrive late.
- Offer a narrowly scoped manual pilot: prepare the missing-document list and follow-up drafts for one Friday, with the bookkeeper reviewing and sending them. Be explicit that no software exists.
- Ask for payment. A **provisional test price of $50 or a locally appropriate equivalent** is an experiment choice, not an established market price. Track delivery time and remaining work for the bookkeeper.

The highest-value uncertainty is whether a reachable payer will pay enough for relief that you can deliver economically. AI becomes relevant after you understand which work can safely be reduced.
