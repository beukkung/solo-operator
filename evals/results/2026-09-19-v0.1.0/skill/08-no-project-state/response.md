Your first step is to **offer a small, paid resume edit directly to recent graduates actively applying for jobs**. Spend this weekend testing payment before building anything.

There is no verified project state or customer evidence yet. Resume editing is a hypothesis; the key unknown is whether reachable graduates will pay for a specific improvement. A graduate may use the service while a parent pays, so record who actually funds it.

Use this five-hour plan. Prices and thresholds below are provisional experiment choices, not market facts.

1. **Define the offer — 30 minutes.** Offer a one-page resume edit for one target role, with tracked changes, a short explanation, and one revision. Choose a price you would actually accept for roughly one hour of work. Limit the test to two customers and promise no hiring outcome.

2. **Find and contact prospects — 90 minutes.** Aim for 10 recent graduates currently applying, through personal introductions or a graduate group that permits offers. Send individual messages:
   > “I’m testing a resume-editing service for recent graduates applying now. For [price], I’ll edit a one-page resume against a target job posting, explain the changes, and include one revision. I’m taking two customers this weekend. Would you like to book?”

3. **Discuss and request payment — 60 minutes.** Ask what they currently do to improve their resume, what feels difficult, and whether they have paid for help before. Confirm scope and delivery timing, then request payment to book. Be transparent about your experience.

4. **Deliver and measure — up to two hours.** For paid bookings, edit manually and track time, revision requests, and customer feedback. Request consent before handling resumes; avoid unnecessary personal data.

**Decision rule:** One payment earns delivery of that small commitment. It does not prove repeatable demand or profit. Replies and compliments alone do not justify building. If nobody pays, distinguish objections about price, trust, and relevance. If too few prospects respond, call the result inconclusive and try another bounded outreach round.
